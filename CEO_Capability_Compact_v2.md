# CEO Capability Compact Framework v2.0
## De Facto Application Design
### "Less Architecture, More Action"

---

## 1. DESIGN PRINCIPLES

| # | Principle | From v1.0 | To v2.0 |
|---|-----------|-----------|---------|
| 1 | **Coverage** | 150 capabilities (encyclopedic) | 30 capabilities (essential) |
| 2 | **Granularity** | 12 tiers (linear ladder) | 3 states (dynamic cycle) |
| 3 | **Evidence** | Academic sources (theory-first) | Failure stories (practice-first) |
| 4 | **Structure** | Static matrix (document) | Living loop (system) |
| 5 | **Success** | Completeness score | Decision quality in real crisis |
| 6 | **Time to value** | 6-12 months assessment | 2-4 weeks application |
| 7 | **Maintenance** | Manual quarterly updates | Self-evolving via usage data |

### The "Rule of 30-20-10"

```
30 Core Capabilities    = What you MUST be good at
20 Derailers            = What will DESTROY you if ignored  
10 Learning Modes       = How you learn FASTEST
─────────────────────────────────────────────
60 Total Elements       = Human working memory limit (~7±2 chunks × 8 domains)
```

> **"A CEO who knows 30 things deeply, avoids 20 traps, and learns 10 ways fast
> will outperform a CEO who knows 150 things shallowly."**

## 3. THE 20 DERAILERS (Lỗ Hổng Phổ Biến)

### 3.1 Why Derailers Matter More Than Capabilities

> **"A CEO with 29/30 capabilities but 1 critical derailer will fail.
> A CEO with 20/30 capabilities and 0 derailers will succeed."**

Research: 70% of CEO failures are due to **derailers**, not lack of capabilities (Source: Center for Creative Leadership).

### 3.2 The 20 Derailers (Organized by Domain)

#### A. STRATEGIC DERAILERS
| # | Derailer | Early Warning Sign | Recovery Action | Real Example |
|---|----------|-------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| S1 | **Analysis Paralysis** | Decisions delayed 3+ times | Set "decision deadline" rule; pre-commit | Nokia's smartphone delay |
| S2 | **Shiny Object Syndrome** | New initiative every quarter | Kill 1 old before starting 1 new | Yahoo's constant pivots |
| S3 | **Last War General** | Apply old playbook to new problem | Hire outsider to challenge assumptions | Blockbuster ignoring Netflix |
| S4 | **Confirmation Bias** | Only read news that agrees | Assign "devil's advocate" to every bet | Lehman Brothers pre-2008 |

#### B. EXECUTION DERAILERS
| # | Derailer | Early Warning Sign | Recovery Action | Real Example |
|---|----------|-------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| E1 | **Micromanagement** | Rewriting team's work | Delegate outcomes, not methods | Elon Musk at Twitter (initial) |
| E2 | **Absence Management** | Travel 80% of time | Set "in-office" minimums | WeWork's Neumann |
| E3 | **Metric Obsession** | Gaming numbers over reality | Add qualitative "smell tests" | Wells Fargo fake accounts |
| E4 | **Process Worship** | Process becomes the goal | Kill process that doesn't serve customer | IBM's bureaucracy |

#### C. PEOPLE DERAILERS
| # | Derailer | Early Warning Sign | Recovery Action | Real Example |
|---|----------|-------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| P1 | **Hiring Clones** | Team looks/thinks like you | Mandate diverse shortlists | Uber's early culture |
| P2 | **Conflict Avoidance** | Issues fester for months | "No surprises" rule; weekly tension scan | Boeing 737 MAX |
| P3 | **Loyalty Trap** | Keep underperformers too long | "Upgrade or exit" timeline | HP's Fiorina era |
| P4 | **Hero Syndrome** | "Only I can fix this" | Force delegation; take vacation | Tesla's Musk (recurring) |

#### D. STAKEHOLDER DERAILERS
| # | Derailer | Early Warning Sign | Recovery Action | Real Example |
|---|----------|-------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| T1 | **Board Blindness** | Surprise board votes | Weekly board pulse check | Travis Kalanick at Uber |
| T2 | **Investor Pandering** | Change strategy for analyst call | 5-year plan > quarterly guidance | Short-termism epidemic |
| T3 | **Media Addiction** | Care more about press than business | "No comment" default for 30 days | Elizabeth Holmes |
| T4 | **Regulatory Arrogance** | Ignore compliance as "bureaucracy" | Hire ex-regulator as advisor | Theranos, FTX |

#### E. FINANCIAL DERAILERS
| # | Derailer | Early Warning Sign | Recovery Action | Real Example |
|---|----------|-------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| F1 | **Cash Blindness** | Focus on P&L, ignore balance sheet | Weekly cash review ritual | Lehman Brothers |
| F2 | **Growth at All Costs** | Burn rate > revenue growth | Unit economics gate for all spend | WeWork, Uber (early) |
| F3 | **Debt Addiction** | Refinance instead of repay | Debt/EBITDA ceiling rule | Sears |
| F4 | **Acquisition Fever** | Buy to fix strategic weakness | Integration plan before deal | AOL-Time Warner |

#### F. PERSONAL DERAILERS
| # | Derailer | Early Warning Sign | Recovery Action | Real Example |
|---|----------|-------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| M1 | **Isolation** | No one tells you bad news | Install "truth-teller" role | Nixon, various dictators |
| M2 | **Hubris** | "I'm the smartest person here" | Mandatory peer CEO group | Dick Fuld (Lehman) |
| M3 | **Burnout Denial** | "I don't need sleep" | Forced sabbatical protocol | Multiple startup founders |
| M4 | **Ethical Drift** | "Everyone does it" | Ethics audit every 6 months | VW emissions scandal |

### 3.3 Derailer Assessment: The "Pre-Mortem"

Instead of asking "What am I good at?", ask:

```
"If I fail as CEO in 3 years, which derailer will be the cause?"

Step 1: Rate yourself 1-5 on each derailer (1 = not a risk, 5 = imminent danger)
Step 2: Top 3 scores = your "derailer portfolio"
Step 3: For each, define:
  - Early warning signal (what you'll watch)
  - Accountability partner (who will call it out)
  - Correction protocol (what you'll do)
```

## 4. THE 10 LEARNING MODES (Cách Học Nhanh)

### 4.1 Why "Learning How to Learn" is a Meta-Capability

A CEO faces new challenges every quarter. The ability to learn fast is more valuable than any specific knowledge.

### 4.2 The 10 Learning Modes

| # | Mode | When to Use | Time to Value | How to Practice | CEO Example |
|---|------|-------------|---------------|-----------------|-------------|
| L1 | **Apprenticeship** | Entering new domain | 6-12 months | Shadow expert 2 days/week | Satya Nadella learning cloud from engineers |
| L2 | **Crisis Immersion** | Must learn fast or die | 2-4 weeks | Full-time focus, no distractions | Alan Mulally at Ford (2006) |
| L3 | **Peer Exchange** | Need perspective, not answers | 1 day | Monthly CEO group, strict confidentiality | YPO/EO forums |
| L4 | **Reverse Mentoring** | Need digital/cultural insight | 1 hour/week | Young employee teaches you | Jack Welch's internet mentor |
| L5 | **Case Study Deep Dive** | Need pattern recognition | 2-3 days | Study 5 similar situations, extract principles | McKinsey-trained CEOs |
| L6 | **Simulation/Role Play** | Need decision practice without cost | 1 day | War games, board simulations | Military-style business exercises |
| L7 | **Teaching** | Need to master something deeply | 3-6 months | Teach it to others (Feynman technique) | Bezos's shareholder letters as teaching |
| L8 | **Experimentation** | Need to test hypothesis | 2-4 weeks | Small bet, measure, iterate | Amazon's "two-pizza teams" |
| L9 | **Reflective Practice** | Need to learn from experience | 30 min/day | Journal, after-action review | Ray Dalio's principles |
| L10 | **Adversarial Learning** | Need to challenge assumptions | 1 hour | Assign someone to argue opposite position | Intel's "disagree and commit" |

### 4.3 Learning Mode Selection Matrix

| Situation | Primary Mode | Secondary Mode | Avoid |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|-------|
| New industry | L1 Apprenticeship | L5 Case Study | L7 Teaching (too slow) |
| Turnaround | L2 Crisis Immersion | L9 Reflective Practice | L3 Peer Exchange (too slow) |
| Digital transformation | L4 Reverse Mentoring | L8 Experimentation | L1 Apprenticeship (you'll never catch up) |
| Board conflict | L3 Peer Exchange | L10 Adversarial Learning | L2 Crisis Immersion (escalates) |
| Scaling operations | L7 Teaching | L8 Experimentation | L4 Reverse Mentoring (wrong domain) |
| Personal burnout | L9 Reflective Practice | L3 Peer Exchange | L2 Crisis Immersion (worsens) |

## 5. THE 3 STATES: KNOW → CAN DO → CAN TEACH

### 5.1 Why 3 States Beat 12 Tiers

| 12 Tiers (v1.0) | 3 States (v2.0) |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| Linear progression | Cyclical deepening |
| Focus on "level" | Focus on "impact" |
| Static assessment | Dynamic demonstration |
| Individual metric | Organizational multiplier |
| Academic precision | Practical clarity |

> **"You don't 'master' a capability. You know it, then you can do it, then you can teach it —
> and then you realize you didn't really know it, and the cycle repeats."**

### 5.2 State Definitions & Evidence

#### STATE 1: "KNOW" (Biết)
| Aspect | Definition | Evidence Required | Typical Time |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|--------------|
| **What** | Can explain concept, name examples, identify when relevant | Explain to peer CEO in 2 minutes | 1-2 weeks reading |
| **Test** | "Teach-back" — explain to someone who doesn't know | They understand and can ask smart questions | — |
| **Trap** | Confusing knowing ABOUT with knowing HOW | Reading 5 books ≠ can do it | — |
| **Transition to Can Do** | Complete 1 real application with feedback | — | — |

#### STATE 2: "CAN DO" (Làm Được)
| Aspect | Definition | Evidence Required | Typical Time |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|--------------|
| **What** | Consistently produce desired outcome in real situations | 3 successful applications | 3-6 months practice |
| **Test** | "Blind evaluation" — outcome judged without knowing who did it | Independent rater scores "good" or better | — |
| **Trap** | Can do in familiar context, fail in new context | Success in 1 industry ≠ transferable | — |
| **Transition to Can Teach** | Someone else learns from watching you | — | — |

#### STATE 3: "CAN TEACH" (Dạy Được)
| Aspect | Definition | Evidence Required | Typical Time |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|--------------|
| **What** | Others learn and apply successfully from your guidance | 2 people reach "Can Do" state | 6-12 months mentoring |
| **Test** | "Successor test" — your replacement outperforms you | Their results exceed yours in same role | — |
| **Trap** | Teaching technique without teaching judgment | Recipe ≠ wisdom | — |
| **Transition** | Return to "Know" with deeper questions | — | — |

### 5.3 The Learning Spiral (Not Ladder)

```
                    ┌─────────────┐
                    │  CAN TEACH  │
                    │  (Dạy Được) │
                    └──────┬──────┘
                           │
              "Realized I   │   "Someone learned"
               didn't know"  │
                           ↓
                    ┌─────────────┐
         ┌─────────│    KNOW     │
         │         │   (Biết)    │
         │         └──────┬──────┘
         │                │
         │   "Deeper      │   "I can explain"
         │    questions"  │
         │                ↓
         │         ┌─────────────┐
         └─────────│   CAN DO    │
                   │ (Làm Được)  │
                   └─────────────┘
                           ↑
              "Applied and it worked"
```

**Key insight:** This is a **spiral**, not a line. Each cycle goes deeper. A CEO who has been through "Know → Can Do → Can Teach → Know" three times on "Bet on the Future" has deeper wisdom than someone who has "completed" 12 tiers once.

## 6. THE DYNAMIC LEARNING LOOP

### 6.1 From Static Matrix to Living System

**v1.0:** Document → Read → Assess → Update document → Repeat (6-month cycle)
**v2.0:** Experience → Reflect → Extract → Apply → Measure → Adapt (daily cycle)

### 6.2 The CEO Learning Loop

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                    CEO LEARNING LOOP                         │
│                     (Weekly Cycle)                          │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                             │
│   ┌──────────┐      ┌──────────┐      ┌──────────┐         │
│   │          │      │          │      │          │         │
│   │EXPERIENCE│─────→│ REFLECT  │─────→│  EXTRACT │         │
│   │          │      │          │      │          │         │
│   └──────────┘      └──────────┘      └─────┬────┘         │
│        ↑                                    │               │
│        │                                    ↓               │
│   ┌────┴────┐                         ┌──────────┐         │
│   │  MEASURE│←────────────────────────│  APPLY   │         │
│   │         │      Did it work?       │          │         │
│   └─────────┘                         └──────────┘         │
│                                                             │
│   Each loop: 1 week                                         │
│   Each capability: 12-24 loops to reach "Can Teach"         │
│   Full spiral: 3-5 years                                    │
│                                                             │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 6.3 Loop Components in Detail

| Component | Question | Tool/Method | Time |
|-----------|----------|-------------|------|
| **EXPERIENCE** | What happened this week? | Decision journal, calendar review | 30 min |
| **REFLECT** | What did I learn? What surprised me? | Structured reflection (What? So What? Now What?) | 30 min |
| **EXTRACT** | What principle/pattern emerges? | Principle card (1 sentence + 1 example) | 15 min |
| **APPLY** | How will I use this next week? | Intent statement + accountability partner | 15 min |
| **MEASURE** | Did my application work? | Outcome tracking + peer feedback | 15 min |

**Total weekly investment: ~2 hours** (vs. v1.0's 20+ hours for assessment)

### 6.4 The "Failure Story" as Primary Learning Unit

Instead of academic papers, v2.0 uses **narrative case studies of failure and recovery**:

| Element | Academic Paper (v1.0) | Failure Story (v2.0) |
|---------|----------------------|---------------------|
| **Format** | Abstract, method, results, discussion | Narrative, emotion, decision points |
| **Engagement** | Cognitive | Emotional + cognitive |
| **Retention** | 10% after 1 week | 65% after 1 week |
| **Transfer** | Theoretical | Immediate applicability |
| **Source** | Journal | Peer CEO, own experience, history |

**Example Failure Story Card:**

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ FAILURE STORY: Nokia's Smartphone Collapse              │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ CEO: Stephen Elop (2010-2014)                           │
│                                                         │
│ THE BET: Windows Phone vs. Android                      │
│                                                         │
│ WHAT HAPPENED:                                          │
│ - Had #1 global market share (40%) in 2008              │
│ - Chose Windows Phone over Android (2011)               │
│ - Market share fell to 3% by 2013                       │
│ - Sold to Microsoft for $7.2B (vs. $250B peak value)    │

│ THE DERAILER: S1 (Analysis Paralysis) + S3 (Last War)   │

│ - Applied "carrier relationship" playbook to app era    │

│ "When platforms shift, relationships don't matter —     │
│  ecosystems do. Choose fast, commit fully, or die."     │

│ RELATED DERAILER: S1, S3                                │
│                                                         │
│ DISCUSSION QUESTIONS:                                   │
│ 1. What would you have done in Elop's position?        │
│ 2. What early signals did Nokia miss?                  │
│ 3. How do you avoid "last war" thinking in your org?   │
```

## 7. IMPLEMENTATION: FROM FRAMEWORK TO PRACTICE

### 7.1 Week 1-2: Self-Diagnosis

| Day | Activity | Output |
|-----|----------|--------|
| 1 | Rate 30 capabilities (Know/Can Do/Can Teach) | Capability map |
| 2 | Rate 20 derailers (1-5 risk level) | Derailer portfolio |
| 3 | Identify top 3 learning modes | Learning plan |
| 4 | Select 3 "failure stories" to study | Story cards |
| 5 | Define accountability partner + weekly check-in | Support system |
| 6-7 | Write "pre-mortem": "How will I fail?" | Risk awareness |

### 7.2 Week 3-4: Focus Sprints

| Sprint | Focus | Method | Duration |
|--------|-------|--------|----------|
| 1 | Top 3 capability gaps | Apprenticeship + Experimentation | 2 weeks |
| 2 | Top 2 derailers | Peer exchange + Reflective practice | 2 weeks |
| 3 | Cross-capability integration | Teaching others | Ongoing |

### 7.3 Month 2-3: Integration

- Weekly learning loop becomes habit
- Monthly peer CEO session
- Quarterly "failure story" deep dive with board
- Annual full reset (re-rate all 30 + 20)

### 7.4 The "CEO Operating System"

```
DAILY (15 min)
├── Morning: Intent (What capability will I practice today?)
└── Evening: Reflection (What happened? What did I learn?)

WEEKLY (2 hours)
├── Monday: Plan (Which learning mode this week?)
├── Wednesday: Mid-week check (Am I on track?)
└── Friday: Extract (What principle emerged?)

MONTHLY (4 hours)
├── Week 1: Capability sprint
├── Week 2: Derailer check
├── Week 3: Peer exchange
└── Week 4: Teaching moment

QUARTERLY (1 day)
├── Pre-mortem update
├── Board feedback session
├── Failure story deep dive
└── Capability re-assessment

ANNUALLY (2 days)
├── Full 30+20 re-evaluation
├── Successor development review
├── Learning mode effectiveness check
└── System redesign (what to add/remove)
```

## 8. MEASUREMENT: DOES THIS WORK?

### 8.1 Leading Indicators (Weekly)

| Indicator | Target | How to Measure |
|-----------|--------|----------------|
| Learning loop completion | 90% weeks | Calendar audit |
| Derailer early warning caught | ≥1/week | Journal review |
| New principle extracted | ≥1/week | Principle card count |
| Peer CEO conversations | ≥2/month | Calendar count |

### 8.2 Lagging Indicators (Quarterly/Annual)

| Indicator | Target | How to Measure |
|-----------|--------|----------------|
| Capability state advancement | +1 state/quarter on 3 capabilities | Self + peer assessment |
| Derailer risk reduction | Top 3 derailers down 1 point | Self-assessment |
| Decision quality | 70%+ "good" calls in retrospect | Decision journal review |
| Team confidence | "Would work for this CEO again" >80% | Anonymous survey |
| Board confidence | "Would rehire" >90% | Board evaluation |
| Business results | TSR, ROIC, employee engagement | Financial + HR data |

### 8.3 The Ultimate Test

> **"If you were hit by a bus tomorrow, would your organization be better off because you were here?"**

This is the "Can Teach" test at the organizational level. If the answer is yes, the system worked.

## 10. THE DESIGNER'S REFLECTION

### What I Learned from Building v1.0

v1.0 was a **masterpiece of architecture** — and a **monument to over-engineering**. It proved that I could design a comprehensive system. It did not prove that the system would make better CEOs.

### What v2.0 Prioritizes

| v1.0 Obsession | v2.0 Priority |
|----------------|---------------|
| Completeness | **Impact** |
| Precision | **Speed** |
| Documentation | **Action** |
| Individual mastery | **Organizational multiplication** |
| Static perfection | **Dynamic adaptation** |

### The Final Principle

> **"The best CEO development system is the one the CEO actually uses — not the one that impresses consultants."**

v2.0 fits on 2 pages. v1.0 needed a server. That difference is the point.
