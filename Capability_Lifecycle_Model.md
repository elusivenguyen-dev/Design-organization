# Capability Lifecycle Model
## 30 Core Capabilities Across 5 Company Stages
### Startup → Growth → Mature → Turnaround → Succession

---

## 1. THE LIFECYCLE ARCHITECTURE

### 1.1 Why Lifecycle Matters

A CEO is not a static role. The same person who excels at finding product-market fit (Startup) may fail at managing a board (Mature) or letting go (Succession). **Capabilities must evolve as the company evolves** — or the CEO must evolve, or be replaced.

| Stage | Duration | Core Challenge | CEO Failure Rate |
|-------|----------|---------------|-----------------|
| **Startup** | 0-2 years | Find product-market fit before cash runs out | 90% of startups fail |
| **Growth** | 2-5 years | Scale without breaking people, process, culture | 70% of scale-ups stall |
| **Mature** | 5-15 years | Innovate while defending; avoid complacency | 50% of mature companies decline |
| **Turnaround** | Crisis (6-24 months) | Survive by cutting deep and moving fast | 80% of turnarounds fail |
| **Succession** | Transition (1-3 years) | Let go while leaving legacy intact | 40% of successions fail |

### 1.2 The Capability Shift Hypothesis

> **"The CEO who got you here won't get you there."**

This is not about CEO quality. It is about **capability-company fit**. Each stage requires a different profile:

| Stage | Dominant Domain | Secondary Domain | Weakest Domain |
|-------|----------------|-----------------|---------------|
| Startup | Strategic (A) + Personal (F) | Execution (B) | Stakeholder (D) |
| Growth | Execution (B) + People (C) | Strategic (A) | Financial (E) |
| Mature | Financial (E) + People (C) | Stakeholder (D) | Strategic (A) |
| Turnaround | Strategic (A) + Stakeholder (D) | Execution (B) | People (C) |
| Succession | People (C) + Personal (F) | Stakeholder (D) | Execution (B) |

## 2. STAGE-BY-STAGE CAPABILITY PROFILES

### 2.1 STARTUP (0-2 years): "Find or Die"

**The Job:** Prove that someone wants what you're building, and that you can build it, before money runs out.

**Top 10 Critical Capabilities:**

| Rank | Capability | Weight | Why Critical | Failure If Weak |
|------|-----------|--------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | **A2 Bet Future** | 5/5 | Startup IS a bet | Wrong bet = death |
| 2 | **A1 Sense Pattern** | 5/5 | See market before it exists | Miss the wave |
| 3 | **A5 Pivot** | 5/5 | Most startups pivot 2-3× | Too late/too proud |
| 4 | **C1 Hire Better** | 5/5 | First 10 hires define culture | Hire wrong, culture breaks |
| 5 | **E3 Manage Cash** | 5/5 | Runway is everything | Run out of cash |
| 6 | **F3 Learn Fast** | 5/5 | New domain every quarter | Can't adapt |
| 7 | **F4 Decide Under Fire** | 5/5 | Incomplete info, high stakes | Paralysis |
| 8 | **B1 Set Pace** | 4/5 | Move fast or die | Too slow |
| 9 | **C3 Build Trust** | 4/5 | Small team, high interdependence | Team breaks |
| 10 | **E2 Allocate Capital** | 4/5 | Limited resources, big choices | Waste on wrong things |

**The Startup CEO Archetype:**
- **Strengths:** Vision, speed, learning, risk tolerance
- **Weaknesses:** Process, governance, financial discipline, stakeholder management
- **Derailer Risk:** S2 (Shiny Object), S4 (Confirmation Bias), F2 (Burnout)
- **When to Replace:** When company needs process more than vision (Growth stage)

**Real Example:**
> **Brian Chesky (Airbnb)** — Sensed pattern (sharing economy), bet on future (strangers in homes), pivoted 3× (airbeds → rooms → experiences), learned fast (designer → CEO), decided under fire (reject $1B acquisition offer). Survived because F3 (Learn Fast) enabled him to grow into the role.

---

### 2.2 GROWTH (2-5 years): "Scale Without Breaking"

**The Job:** Multiply revenue, team, and operations while maintaining culture and quality.

**Top 10 Critical Capabilities:**

| Rank | Capability | Weight | Why Critical | Failure If Weak |
|------|-----------|--------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | **B1 Set Pace** | 5/5 | Organization must accelerate | Stall |
| 2 | **C1 Hire Better** | 5/5 | Need 10× people, maintain quality | Hire wrong, culture breaks |
| 3 | **C3 Build Trust** | 5/5 | New people don't know each other | Silos, politics |
| 4 | **A1 Sense Pattern** | 4/5 | Market is changing as you scale | Miss next wave |
| 5 | **A2 Bet Future** | 4/5 | Need new bets while scaling current | Underinvest in future |
| 6 | **A4 Build Moat** | 4/5 | Competitors copy as you prove market | Commoditized |
| 7 | **B2 Follow Through** | 4/5 | More commitments, more complexity | Drop balls |
| 8 | **B3 Simplify** | 4/5 | Complexity grows with scale | Chaos |
| 9 | **B4 Measure** | 4/5 | Need to know what's working | Fly blind |
| 10 | **B5 Fix Process** | 4/5 | Ad-hoc breaks at scale | Breakdown |

**The Growth CEO Archetype:**
- **Strengths:** Operational excellence, hiring, process building, execution
- **Weaknesses:** Strategic vision (if not founder), financial sophistication, board management
- **Derailer Risk:** E1 (Micromanagement), E3 (Metric Obsession), P1 (Hiring Clones)
- **When to Replace:** When company needs efficiency over speed (Mature stage)

**Real Example:**
> **Sheryl Sandberg at Facebook** — B1 (Set Pace), C1 (Hire Better), B3 (Simplify), B4 (Measure). Took Facebook from 500 to 35,000 employees. But note: she was COO, not CEO. Zuckerberg provided A (Strategic) and F (Personal). **Growth stage often needs a strong #2 to complement the founder.**

**The Founder vs. Professional CEO Tension:**
```
Startup: Founder CEO dominates (A + F)
Growth: Need professional CEO or strong COO (B + C)
  → If founder can't grow: Replace or add COO
  → If founder can grow: Rare (Bezos, Zuckerberg, Nadella)
```

---

### 2.3 MATURE (5-15 years): "Innovate or Die Slowly"

**The Job:** Defend market position, optimize efficiency, innovate for next cycle, and prepare succession.

**Top 10 Critical Capabilities:**

| Rank | Capability | Weight | Why Critical | Failure If Weak |
|------|-----------|--------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | **A4 Build Moat** | 5/5 | Defend against competitors | Eroded margins |
| 2 | **B2 Follow Through** | 5/5 | Large org, many commitments | Execution gaps |
| 3 | **B3 Simplify** | 5/5 | Complexity accumulates | Bureaucracy |
| 4 | **B4 Measure** | 5/5 | Need to optimize | Waste |
| 5 | **B5 Fix Process** | 5/5 | Processes decay | Inefficiency |
| 6 | **C2 Hard Conversations** | 5/5 | Dead wood accumulates | Mediocrity |
| 7 | **C4 Shape Culture** | 5/5 | Culture erodes over time | Toxic culture |
| 8 | **D2 Manage Board** | 5/5 | Board becomes more active | Governance crisis |
| 9 | **E1 Read Numbers** | 5/5 | Financial complexity grows | Miss problems |
| 10 | **E2 Allocate Capital** | 5/5 | Large capital base | Misallocation |

**The Mature CEO Archetype:**
- **Strengths:** Efficiency, governance, financial discipline, stakeholder management
- **Weaknesses:** Speed, innovation, risk tolerance, learning agility
- **Derailer Risk:** S3 (Last War), E4 (Process Worship), M2 (Hubris), M1 (Isolation)
- **When to Replace:** When disruption threatens and CEO can't adapt (Turnaround)

**Real Example:**
> **Tim Cook at Apple** — A4 (Build Moat: ecosystem), B2-B5 (Operational excellence), E1-E2 (Financial discipline). Not a visionary like Jobs, but built the machine that makes Apple's scale possible. **Mature stage needs operational excellence more than vision.**

**The Complacency Trap:**
```
Year 5-10: CEO is hero, company is dominant
Year 10-15: CEO stops learning (F3), stops sensing (A1)
Year 15+: Disruption arrives, CEO is "last war general" (S3)
  → Kodak, Nokia, Blockbuster, Sears
```

---

### 2.4 TURNAROUND (Crisis): "Cut Deep, Move Fast, Survive"

**The Job:** Stop the bleeding, restore confidence, and create a viable path forward — in 6-24 months.

**Top 10 Critical Capabilities:**

| Rank | Capability | Weight | Why Critical | Failure If Weak |
|------|-----------|--------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | **A3 Kill Cow** | 5/5 | Must cut sacred cows | Half-measures fail |
| 2 | **A5 Pivot** | 5/5 | Change direction fast | Too slow |
| 3 | **B1 Set Pace** | 5/5 | Crisis demands urgency | Denial |
| 4 | **B2 Follow Through** | 5/5 | Commitments must be kept | Credibility loss |
| 5 | **B3 Simplify** | 5/5 | Complexity kills in crisis | Confusion |
| 6 | **C2 Hard Conversations** | 5/5 | Must fire people, cut products | Avoidance |
| 7 | **C4 Shape Culture** | 5/5 | Culture must change fast | Old culture persists |
| 8 | **D1 Read Room** | 5/5 | Stakeholders are panicking | Miss signals |
| 9 | **D2 Manage Board** | 5/5 | Board may fire CEO | Lose board |
| 10 | **D3 Speak to Power** | 5/5 | Investors, media, regulators need confidence | Lose support |

**The Turnaround CEO Archetype:**
- **Strengths:** Decisiveness, courage, communication, stakeholder management
- **Weaknesses:** Long-term thinking, people development, culture building
- **Derailer Risk:** S1 (Analysis Paralysis), P2 (Conflict Avoidance), M2 (Hubris)
- **When to Replace:** After stabilization (back to Growth or Mature profile)

**Real Example:**
> **Alan Mulally at Ford (2006-2014)** — A3 (Kill: sold Jaguar, Land Rover, Volvo), A5 (Pivot: "One Ford" strategy), B1-B3 (Pace, follow-through, simplify), D1-D3 (Read room, manage board, speak to power). Didn't take government bailout. **Turnaround CEOs are often outsiders who can see what insiders can't.**

**The Turnaround Paradox:**
```
Turnaround requires:
  → Cutting people (C2) while building trust (C3)
  → Moving fast (B1) while being careful (E5)
  → Taking heat (D5) while staying grounded (F5)
  → Killing cows (A3) while maintaining hope (C4)

This is why 80% of turnarounds fail. The contradictions are extreme.
```

---

### 2.5 SUCCESSION (Transition): "Let Go, Leave Legacy"

**The Job:** Prepare the organization for life after you, transfer knowledge and relationships, and exit with dignity.

**Top 10 Critical Capabilities:**

| Rank | Capability | Weight | Why Critical | Failure If Weak |
|------|-----------|--------|-------------|----------------|
| 1 | **A3 Kill Cow** | 5/5 | Must kill "CEO dependency" | Organization can't function without you |
| 2 | **C3 Build Trust** | 5/5 | Successor needs trust to lead | Power vacuum |
| 3 | **C4 Shape Culture** | 5/5 | Culture must survive transition | Culture breaks |
| 4 | **C5 Develop Leaders** | 5/5 | Need viable successor | No successor |
| 5 | **D1 Read Room** | 5/5 | Stakeholders anxious about transition | Misread signals |
| 6 | **D2 Manage Board** | 5/5 | Board manages succession | Board conflict |
| 7 | **D4 Build Coalitions** | 5/5 | Align stakeholders around successor | Coalition breaks |
| 8 | **F1 Know Bias** | 5/5 | Recognize attachment to role | Won't let go |
| 9 | **F5 Stay Grounded** | 5/5 | Ego fights letting go | Ego destroys legacy |
| 10 | **A4 Build Moat** | 4/5 | Ensure company is defensible post-exit | Company weakens |

**The Succession CEO Archetype:**
- **Strengths:** Self-awareness, humility, long-term thinking, people development
- **Weaknesses:** Speed, operational intensity, risk-taking
- **Derailer Risk:** M2 (Hubris), M1 (Isolation), P4 (Hero Syndrome)
- **When to Replace:** When successor is ready and CEO won't leave

**Real Example:**
> **Bill Gates at Microsoft (2000)** — C5 (Developed Ballmer, then Nadella), A3 (Killed "Gates as CEO" dependency), F1 (Knew he was better as technologist than CEO), F5 (Stayed grounded through foundation). **Succession is the ultimate test of ego management.**

**The Succession Failure Modes:**
```
Mode 1: No successor developed (C5 weak)
  → Forced to hire external CEO who doesn't know culture
  → Example: Yahoo's revolving door

Mode 2: CEO won't leave (F1, F5 weak)
  → Board fires CEO, messy exit
  → Example: Travis Kalanick at Uber

Mode 3: Successor not ready (C5 weak)
  → Premature transition, company suffers
  → Example: Ron Johnson at JCPenney

Mode 4: Succession is surprise (D2 weak)
  → Health crisis, death, scandal
  → Example: Steve Jobs at Apple (health)
```

## 3. THE CEO ARC: WHAT CHANGES, WHAT STAYS, WHAT RETURNS

### 3.1 The Three Curves

```
Years 1-2 (Startup):   Strategic (A) peaks, Execution (B) low, Personal (F) high
Years 3-5 (Growth):    Execution (B) peaks, Strategic (A) dips, Personal (F) steady
Years 6-10 (Mature):   Execution (B) high, Strategic (A) low, Personal (F) dips
Years 11-15 (Turnaround): Strategic (A) returns, Execution (B) high, Personal (F) critical
Years 16+ (Succession): People (C) peaks, Strategic (A) low, Personal (F) highest
```

### 3.2 What Stays Constant

| Capability | Why Constant | Risk If It Fluctuates |
|-----------|-------------|----------------------|
| **F5 Stay Grounded** | Ego is always a threat | Hubris at any stage kills |
| **F2 Manage Energy** | Burnout is always possible | Energy crash at wrong moment |
| **E3 Manage Cash** | Cash is always oxygen | Run out = death at any stage |
| **C3 Build Trust** | Trust is always the foundation | Broken trust = broken organization |

### 3.3 What Returns

| Capability | First Peak | Returns At | Why Returns |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|-------------|
| **A1 Sense Pattern** | Startup | Turnaround | Disruption requires pattern recognition |
| **A2 Bet Future** | Startup | Mature | Need new bets when current matures |
| **A5 Pivot** | Startup | Turnaround | Crisis requires direction change |
| **F1 Know Bias** | Always | Succession | Letting go requires self-awareness |
| **C5 Develop Leaders** | Growth | Succession | Succession is the payoff of development |

## 4. THE TRANSITION ZONES: WHEN TO SWITCH

### 4.1 Transition Signals

| From | To | Signal | Action |
|------|-----|--------|--------|
| Startup → Growth | Product-market fit achieved, revenue growing 3×/year | Add COO or replace CEO |
| Growth → Mature | Growth slows to <20%/year, market share stable | Shift from speed to efficiency |
| Mature → Turnaround | Revenue decline, margin compression, talent leaving | Bring in turnaround CEO |
| Any → Succession | CEO tenure >10 years, successor ready, CEO tired | Plan transition |

### 4.2 The "Stay Too Long" Trap

| Stage | Typical Overstay | Cost |
|-------|-----------------|------|
| Startup | Founder stays through Growth, can't scale | Company stalls or fails |
| Growth | Growth CEO stays into Mature, can't optimize | Margins erode |
| Mature | Mature CEO stays through Disruption, can't adapt | Company disrupted |
| Turnaround | Turnaround CEO stays into Growth, can't build | Culture too harsh |

**The Rule:** *"A CEO's greatest strength becomes their greatest weakness when the stage changes."*

## 5. THE LIFECYCLE DERAILER MAP

### 5.1 Stage-Specific Derailer Risks

| Stage | Highest Risk Derailers | Why | Prevention |
|-------|----------------------|-----|-----------|
| **Startup** | S2 (Shiny Object), S4 (Confirmation), F2 (Burnout) | Too many bets, wrong market, founder exhaustion | Focus, validate, rest |
| **Growth** | E1 (Micromanagement), E3 (Metric Obsession), P1 (Clones) | Can't let go, game numbers, hire comfort | Delegate, quality metrics, diversity |
| **Mature** | S3 (Last War), E4 (Process Worship), M2 (Hubris), M1 (Isolation) | Apply old playbook, process over purpose, ego, no truth-tellers | Outside advisors, customer visits, peer CEO group |
| **Turnaround** | S1 (Analysis Paralysis), P2 (Conflict Avoidance), M2 (Hubris) | Wait too long, avoid cuts, "I can fix it alone" | Decision deadlines, forced conversations, team |
| **Succession** | M2 (Hubris), M1 (Isolation), P4 (Hero Syndrome) | "I'm irreplaceable", no feedback, "only I can do this" | Succession planning, peer group, forced delegation |

## 6. THE LIFECYCLE ASSESSMENT TOOL

### 6.1 CEO-Company Fit Scorecard

| Question | Score 1-5 | Evidence |
|----------|----------|----------|
| Does the company's stage match the CEO's dominant capabilities? | ___ | Capability profile vs. stage needs |
| Is the CEO learning the capabilities needed for the next stage? | ___ | F3 (Learn Fast) evidence |
| Is the CEO aware of stage-specific derailer risks? | ___ | F1 (Know Bias) evidence |
| Does the board understand the stage transition risks? | ___ | D2 (Manage Board) evidence |
| Is there a succession plan for the next stage? | ___ | C5 (Develop Leaders) evidence |

**Total Score:**
- 25-20: Strong fit, monitor
- 19-15: Moderate fit, develop
- 14-10: Weak fit, plan transition
- 9-5: Danger, act now

### 6.2 The "Stage Mismatch" Diagnostic

| Symptom | Likely Mismatch | Solution |
|---------|----------------|----------|
| "CEO is brilliant but company is chaos" | Startup CEO in Growth | Add COO or replace |
| "CEO is efficient but company is stagnant" | Growth CEO in Mature | Add innovation leader or replace |
| "CEO is cautious but market is shifting" | Mature CEO in Turnaround | Replace with turnaround CEO |
| "CEO won't delegate anything" | Any stage, P4 (Hero Syndrome) | Coach or replace |
| "CEO has no successor after 10 years" | Succession failure | Board intervention |

## 7. THE META-LESSON: LIFECYCLE AS A CAPABILITY ITSELF

### 7.1 The Ultimate Capability

> **"The ability to recognize which stage you're in — and which capabilities are needed — is itself a capability."**

This is **meta-capability L11: "Read the Lifecycle"** (not in the 30, but implied by F3 + A1 + D1).

### 7.2 The Lifecycle-Aware CEO

| CEO | Lifecycle Awareness | Outcome |
|-----|-------------------|---------|
| **Bezos** | Built Amazon through Startup→Growth→Mature, adapted capabilities | Amazon still growing after 25+ years |
| **Gates** | Recognized Startup→Growth transition, hired Ballmer, then left | Microsoft survived transition |
| **Jobs** | Didn't recognize Mature stage needs (initially), returned with new capabilities | Apple turnaround |
| **Welch** | Recognized Mature→Growth at GE, transformed company | GE peak (though later decline) |
| **Fiorina** | Didn't recognize HP's stage, applied wrong capabilities | Fired, company damaged |
